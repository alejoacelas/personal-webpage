At sixteen, I stumbled onto effective altruism and something shifted permanently. The idea that you could be rigorous about doing good — that you could treat impact like an engineering problem — rewired the way I thought about everything.

At university, I studied economics — the discipline that takes messy human behavior and tries to find the levers. I co-founded the EA chapter, gathered people who cared about the same impossible questions, and started building.

In Oxford, the questions got sharper. I researched AI governance, lived in a group house full of people trying to steer the future, and learned that the gap between understanding a problem and actually moving the needle is where most good intentions go to die.

In Bogotá, I came home. Not to rest — to build. Cliver, ML4Good, consulting work that puts me at the intersection of AI capability and the institutions trying to keep up. The problems I care about don't have geographic boundaries, but the work has to happen somewhere, and I chose here.
