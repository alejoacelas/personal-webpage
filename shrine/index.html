THE PATH

At 16, I discovered Effective Altruism — the idea that we could use evidence and reason to figure out how to do the most good. It changed the trajectory of everything that followed.

I studied economics, drawn to the discipline's rigor in modeling complex systems. But I quickly realized that the problems I cared about most — catastrophic risks, emerging technologies, global coordination failures — sat at the edges of any single field.

I helped build the Oxford EA chapter, connecting with a community of people who took these questions as seriously as I did. It was there that biosecurity and AI governance moved from abstract concerns to the center of my work.

Now based in Bogotá, I work at the intersection of AI, biosecurity, and policy — building tools, advising institutions, and trying to close the gap between what technology can do and what our governance systems are prepared for.
